01The problem

Six services. Six contacts. Six invoices.

When each service comes from a different vendor, each vendor brings its own phone number, its own invoice and its own idea of what “done” means. Someone on your team becomes the switchboard.

Fragmented

48,000+

licensed private-security agencies supply India’s roughly 10 million security workers.4 Choosing one, and checking its papers, is work your team repeats for every service.

Fragile

56.8%

of surveyed security employers describe attrition as high.2 When someone leaves, somebody has to notice, arrange cover and tell you. Without a partner who owns that, it is you.

Costly in-house

₹8 lakh

is the average yearly pay reported for administration managers in India (a salary-site estimate).5 We found no reliable figure for hours spent chasing vendors, so we will not invent one. The cost is real, and no vendor invoice shows it.

The paperwork behind a multi-vendor office

  1. Writing scopes and comparing quotes
  2. Checking each vendor’s licences and papers
  3. Matching invoices to attendance and deliveries
  4. Drawing up rosters and finding replacements
  5. Inspecting quality and chasing complaints
  6. Forecasting pantry demand and reordering stock
  7. Booking preventive maintenance and chasing repairs
  8. Tracking PF, ESI, licence and insurance renewals
  9. Reporting on service levels, deductions and renewals

Not every office does all nine. Each one is time that appears on no vendor invoice.

03How it works

From first call to first statement in four steps.

    Tell us about your office

08How we use AI

Where AI helps, and where a person decides.

We are building the platform around AI models such as Claude. The rule we design to: AI reads, drafts and flags, and a person on our team approves anything that reaches a client or a vendor.

  • Planned

    Matching invoices to attendance

    Vendors send invoices, attendance sheets and PF and ESI challans as scanned PDFs and phone photos. Claude reads them, matches billed shifts and rates to attendance and the agreed contract, and flags each difference with the page and line that shows it.

  • Planned

    Watching licences and policies

    Claude reads security licences, food licences and insurance policies, pulls out the number, the validity dates and the premises covered, and checks them against the vendor record. Anything close to expiry, or that does not match, is flagged early with a drafted reminder for our team to send.

  • Planned

    Checking site photos against a checklist

    Housekeeping and pantry supervisors send photos from site. Claude compares them with the agreed checklist, such as bins emptied, floors dry and shelves stocked, and marks what is missing. A person reviews every failed item.

  • Planned

    Tickets in the language people use

    Site staff and vendor supervisors often report problems in Kannada, Hindi, Marathi or English, sometimes with only a photo. Claude turns the message into a clear ticket in English, suggests the vendor and the priority, and keeps the original message beside it.

  • Planned

    Explaining why a bill moved

    When a month's total changes, Claude drafts a short note in plain words, with each sentence tied to the invoice line, attendance record or approved request behind it. A sentence it cannot tie to a record is left out.

  • Planned

    Comparing vendor quotes like for like

    Vendor quotes arrive as PDFs and emails in different formats. Claude turns each one into the same line items, so two quotes can be compared on the same scope. A person checks the comparison before it goes to a client.

Claude is an AI model made by Anthropic. Citizen Spacezs is an independent company.
